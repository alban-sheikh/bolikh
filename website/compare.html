Where Bolikh stands

What it does well

It is free with no limits, open source, works offline, and is the same app on Mac, Windows and Linux. The text comes out tidy: no “um”, numbers as digits, and commands such as “new line” and “scratch that”.

What it doesn’t do

No phone app, no AI rewriting, no transcribing of audio files, and no Hindi or other languages outside its 25 European ones. Intel Macs aren’t supported yet. If you need one of these, an app in the table will suit you better.

And the dictation built into your computer

Macs have dictation built in, and it is fine for a quick sentence. Windows has voice typing (Windows key + H), which uses Microsoft’s online speech service. Most Linux desktops have nothing built in. Bolikh is for people who write by voice often: you hold a key for as long as you talk, fillers are removed, you can teach it your own words, and it keeps a history of what you said.

Questions

Is Bolikh built on Whisper?

No. Bolikh uses NVIDIA’s Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 speech model, which runs on your own computer. Whisper is a different speech model, made by OpenAI. Several apps with “whisper” in their name are built on it.

Which of these dictation apps are free?

Bolikh and Handy are free with nothing held back. Superwhisper, Wispr Flow and MacWhisper have free plans with limits or fewer features. VoiceInk is a one-time purchase, or free if you build it yourself.

Which ones work without internet?

Bolikh, Handy, MacWhisper, and Superwhisper and VoiceInk with their local models. Wispr Flow needs a connection.

Which ones run on Linux?

Of the apps on this page, Bolikh and Handy.
